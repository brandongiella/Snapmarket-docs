## What this is

Voice to Market. A recording arrives and becomes the show's full content package, published everywhere it belongs.

The exact set is defined per show, because a fund's package and a contractor's package should not look the same. A typical one includes the edited episode, a long-form written piece, a newsletter, a set of questions and answers drawn from the conversation, short clips for social, an announcement post, show notes, and the transcript.

Everything in that list comes from the same forty minutes. That is the whole idea, and it is why this piece exists as a single unit rather than seven separate requests.

## Why it is one piece and not seven

A practical consequence worth understanding, because it affects how work gets tracked.

If a deliverable originates from a transcript, it belongs to this piece regardless of what it looks like at the end. A newsletter derived from an episode is part of the episode package, not a separate email marketing piece. A blog post derived from an episode is part of the package, not a separate writing piece.

The reason is not bookkeeping. It is that deriving seven things from one conversation is a fundamentally cheaper operation than producing seven things from nothing, and pricing them as if they were the same would misrepresent both.

## What we need from you

The recording, and a review pass.

On the recording: record a bit more than you think you need, do not stop for small mistakes, and resist scripting. The pieces that travel furthest are almost always the moments where someone said something slightly more candid than they had planned to.

On review: a single consolidated approval, ideally within a couple of days. This is the step that determines whether the show publishes on cadence, and a package sitting in an approval queue for a week is how a fortnightly show becomes a monthly one without anybody deciding that.

Where your industry is compliance-sensitive, tell us at the start rather than at review. Certain claims need particular handling, and knowing that up front is far cheaper than a rewrite.

## What you will have

The full package for the episode, published to your feed, your website, your social accounts, and your list, with tracking on every link so the [Scale layer](/scale/overview) can see which pieces actually did something.

## How long it takes

About seven days from recording to fully published, assuming review comes back promptly. Technically complex or compliance-sensitive episodes run longer, and we will say so when we take one on.

## What sits outside the fee

Hosting, editing, publishing, and email platform subscriptions, billed at cost, in accounts you own.

## A note on sources

This process does not require a podcast. Any recording works—a webinar, a conference talk, an internal briefing, a customer conversation you had permission to record, or a voice memo you made in the car because you had a thought. If it is a recording of someone saying something true, it can become a package.
