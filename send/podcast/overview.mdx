The podcast is not a channel we happen to like. It is the input to the rest of Chapter 3.

Here is the problem it solves. A content program that generates each piece independently—a blog from a blank page on Monday, a newsletter from a blank page on Wednesday, five social posts from a blank page on Friday—asks a founder for the same scarce thing over and over, which is original thinking on demand, and it collapses within a quarter. Every time. It also happens to be expensive to produce.

A recorded conversation asks for that thinking once, in the format humans are best at, and everything else is derived from it. Forty minutes of you talking about something you actually know produces a written piece, a newsletter, a set of clips, show notes, and a month of social material. The founder's job becomes showing up and being interesting for forty minutes, which is a job most leaders can actually do.

## Podcast is a loose word here

It does not have to be a podcast in the conventional sense, and for several partners it is not.

The format is a recorded conversation on a schedule. That can be an interview show, a solo commentary, a recorded internal briefing, a webinar series, or a monthly conversation with a customer. What matters is that it is recorded, that it happens regularly, and that it captures a real person thinking out loud rather than reading a script.

We help you pick a format you will sustain rather than the one that sounds most impressive at kickoff.

## The honest prerequisite

This section asks something of you that no other section does, and it is worth being direct about it.

Someone has to be willing to be on record, regularly, for long time. Usually the founder, executive, or SME. If nobody in your company wants that, this is the wrong bet, and we would rather establish that in the Prologue than four months into a show that slowly dies.

The question we ask in the thesis conversation is deliberately blunt: are you willing to be on camera every week for a year? A no is a perfectly good answer. It changes the bet rather than ending the conversation.
