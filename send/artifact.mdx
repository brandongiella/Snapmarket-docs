Chapter 3 closes when your podcast has published on its stated cadence for up to 10 episodes without a miss.

Not when it launches. Launching is a project with a deadline and everyone shows up for it. Sixty days later, when the novelty is gone and the recording is competing with a full calendar and nobody outside the company has said anything encouraging yet, is the point at which we learn whether the thing is real.

## Why this is the line

Because cadence is the multiplier, and cadence is the thing that fails.

The overwhelming majority of company podcasts stop between episode four and episode nine. They do not stop because the content was bad. They stop because nobody built a routine that survives a busy month, and because the audience had not yet grown large enough to create any obligation to continue.

Ten episodes on schedule means the routine exists. The recording is on the calendar and gets protected. Approvals move. The process runs without heroics. Once that is true, the show tends to keep going for years, and everything downstream of it—the writing, the newsletter, the social—has a reliable source.

We made it an artifact because it is the honest test of whether Chapter 3 has actually started.

## What counts

Published on the cadence you set, without a gap. We would rather you chose a cadence you will clear comfortably than an ambitious one you have to rescue. The number of episodes is not the point. The unbroken line is.

## What happens after

Nothing changes and that is the intention. The chapter is marked complete and the machine keeps running, and from here Send compounds for as long as the partnership lasts.

What changes is what we watch. Before the artifact the question is whether the routine will hold. After it, the question moves to what the work is producing—which episodes performed, which topics generated replies, which pieces show up in the path of people who eventually became customers. That is the [Scale layer’s](/scale/overview) work, and by this point it usually has enough clean data to say something.

## If it does not hold

Sometimes it does not, and it is worth saying what happens then.

A cadence that keeps slipping is information, not a failure to be managed. Usually it means one of three things: the format asks too much of the host, the cadence was set optimistically, or the person on the show does not actually want to be doing it.

All three are fixable, and all three are cheaper to name in month two than to discover in month eight. The audit will raise it rather than describe a stalling show as steady.
