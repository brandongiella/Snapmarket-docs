Every partnership ends. Most agency relationships end badly, not because anyone behaved badly but because nobody planned for the ending, and so it arrives as a gradual cooling followed by an email and a folder nobody can open.

We treat the ending as a chapter with its own work and its own capstone. It is the last thing we do and it is the part of the manuscript most likely to determine what you say about us afterward.

## What happens

<Steps>
  <Step title="Final partnership review">
    A proper look back across everything we built. Every capstone, what each chapter produced, what worked and what did not, and where the honest gaps are. Written the way the audits were written, which is to say without flattering anybody.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A verdict on the thesis">
    The claim we opened with is rendered confirmed, disconfirmed, or amended, with the full stack of revisions behind it. This is the section people are most surprised by and it is the one we would least want to skip.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Asset and credential handover">
    Everything transferred into your control. Source files, documents, the site, the show, the accounts. Written documentation of what exists and where it lives, so nothing is stranded behind a login only we had.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The referral conversation, and an open door">
    A conversation about who else this might suit, and a genuine invitation to stay in touch. Several of our best partnerships came from someone we had stopped working with.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The verdict

This is the part that makes the ending mean something.

Because the [thesis](/method/thesis) was written as a claim that could be wrong, and because every audit measured against it, there is an actual answer at the end rather than a set of impressions. The bet either held, or it did not, or it turned out to be partly right in a way worth naming precisely.

A disconfirmed thesis is not a failed partnership. It is frequently the most valuable thing you leave with, because you now know something specific and expensive about your own market that you did not know before, and you know it with evidence rather than as a feeling.

The stack of revisions behind the verdict is the intellectual history of the work—what we believed at the start, where we were wrong, what we learned that changed the argument. That stack is what makes a handover feel like a finished book rather than a folder of files.

## What is already yours, and what has to move

The handover splits cleanly in two, because of a line we draw at the beginning.

Anything that represents you in the world was always registered to you. Your domain, your analytics, your Search Console, your YouTube channel, your social handles, and any platform you already held when we met. For all of that there is nothing to transfer. You remove our access and the history, the settings, and the audience stay exactly where they were.

The production tooling is different. The platforms we record, edit, publish, and schedule in run on our subscriptions, which is what lets a partnership start on day one instead of after a procurement conversation. Those end when the partnership does. If you want to keep working in them, you set up your own accounts and we port everything across—content, settings, and configuration, as completely as each platform allows. We tell you which ones you actually need, with current pricing, before your last month rather than after it. Some transfers are clean and some are partial, and we say which is which rather than letting you discover it alone.

We draw the line there on purpose. An agency that holds your audience, your domain, or your data has leverage it should not have, and a client who stays because leaving would be painful is not a client who is choosing you. Holding the tooling is a convenience we carry. Holding your identity would be something else.

## A pause is not an ending

Sometimes a partner steps back for a season rather than leaving. Budget cycles, a leadership change, a year where the company needs to focus elsewhere.

That is a pause, and we treat it differently. Context stays intact, the files stay organized, the thesis stays on record, and coming back does not mean starting over. We would rather hold a relationship open than force a clean break for the sake of tidiness.

## What ends the Epilogue

A completed final review and a documented handover. At that point the manuscript is finished, and you own all of it.
