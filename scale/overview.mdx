## Translators, not a data team

We do not pretend to be data scientists. We are translators. We read messy data from six platforms and turn it into a clear account of what is happening, in language you can take to a board without an interpreter. What you need is someone who explains the funnel plainly and shows what is working without burying you in numbers that flatter everyone and inform nobody.

You are not expected to log into a dashboard and work out what it means. That is our job. You get a written account of what happened and what we think it implies, and the dashboard sits underneath it if you ever want to check our work.

So our job is to make your data legible, not to invent more of it.

## How often this happens

The data is reviewed every month and we act on it every month. The strategy itself is not on a schedule. It can hold for years, and often should, because a plan revised every thirty days never runs long enough to tell you whether it worked. What changes a strategy is evidence, not the calendar.

## What this layer asks of you

Two things, and they are the only two.

Context we do not have. A pricing change, a new salesperson, a large customer who signed through a personal relationship. Missing context is the most common cause of a misread month.

And a decision on each recommendation. Every one is tagged to a piece of work, so agreeing means it gets scheduled and declining means we will not raise it again without new evidence. A review received without a response has become a formality, and formalities are how partnerships drift.

## The point of all of it

The standing [thesis](/method/thesis) is the claim. Scale is the apparatus for finding out whether it is true.

Without the thesis, measurement produces a stream of numbers with no argument behind them, and any month can be narrated as a good one. With it, there is a fixed claim on record, and every audit is a report on whether reality is cooperating.
