Every month of the partnership you receive an audit. It is Scale's one recurring artifact and the most important document we produce after the thesis.

It is written as an editor's letter rather than assembled as a dashboard export. Prose, an argument, a position. Something a person wrote after looking at the evidence and forming a view, which is a different object from a set of charts with captions.

## What is in it

A reading of every chapter of your manuscript, each carrying one of four status words, used consistently so that a word means the same thing in September as it did in March.

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Strong | Performing at or above benchmark. Protect it and do more of it |
| Steady | Working, but not optimized. Refine when there is capacity |
| Weak | Underperforming or causing concern. Intervention required |
| Not yet running | Has not started. Either still in baseline or awaiting a scope decision |

Alongside the statuses: the results of last month's declared [experiments](/scale/experiments), successes and failures reported the same way. The cohort and transition numbers with a note wherever one is not yet trustworthy. What we propose for next month, each recommendation tagged to the piece of work that would deliver it. And a check against your standing [thesis](/method/thesis)—is the claim still holding.

## What makes it different from a report

The [performance report](/system/analytics/performance-reporting) tells you what the instruments read. The audit tells you what we think it means and what we intend to do about it.

The second is harder to write and easier to fake, which is why the discipline around it matters. An audit that does not name at least one thing that is not working is an audit that has stopped being useful, and we would rather write an uncomfortable sentence in month four than have an uncomfortable meeting in month eleven.

## During the baseline

For the first sixty to ninety days the audit covers instrumentation health and qualitative observation only. No lift claims, no comparisons, no numbers presented as findings. See [the baseline period](/scale/baseline) for why.

Those early audits are shorter and they are still worth reading. They are where the scope conversations happen, where gaps get named, and where the sequence gets adjusted while adjusting is still cheap.

## What we need from you

Read it, and push back on it. An audit that is received without response is an audit that has become a formality, and formalities are how partnerships drift.

Context we do not have. A pricing change, a new salesperson, a large customer who signed through a personal relationship, a competitor doing something disruptive. Missing context is the most common cause of a misread month.

And a decision on the recommendations. Each one is tagged to a piece of work, so agreeing to it means work gets scheduled and declining it means we do not raise it again next month without new evidence.

## Why it is called the audit

Because an audit is the thing you cannot argue with afterward. It is written down, dated, and it says what it says.

A year of audits is an honest account of what a partnership did, including the parts that did not work. That is the record the [Epilogue](/epilogue) draws on when it renders a verdict on your thesis, and it is what makes a handover feel like a finished book rather than a folder of files.
