Your website is the only significant marketing asset you fully own. Every other surface is rented—a platform can change its algorithm, its terms, or its mind, and your audience there goes with it. This is why the website is Chapter 2’s artifact rather than one item among several.

It is also the place where a settled message either survives contact with reality or does not. A messaging document that reads beautifully and then produces a homepage nobody can navigate has not actually been settled. The site is the first honest test.

## What we build in

Framer, by default.

The reasoning is not fashion. Framer sites are fast to build, fast to load, and—critically—editable by your team without a developer. A site that requires us for every copy change becomes a bottleneck by month four, and a bottleneck you pay for is worse than one you do not.

## The thing that decides the timeline

Website projects run long for one reason more than any other, and it is not design. It is review structured as a stream of individual reactions rather than a small number of consolidated responses.

Two rounds of feedback, each gathered from everyone who has an opinion and delivered as one document or set of comments, keeps a build to six or eight weeks. The same total volume of feedback arriving as forty messages across nine weeks does not produce a better site.

<Note>
  We will ask you at the start who the reviewers are and how feedback will be consolidated. It is worth answering carefully.
