There are two ways to work with us, and they’re genuinely different things rather than two sizes of the same thing.

A **project** is one bounded piece of work with a defined end. A website. An updated visual identity. A messaging workshop. You pay, we do the work, we ship it, and we move on. This is what most agencies do, and when it’s what you need, it’s the honest answer.

A **partnership** is a manuscript. We become your marketing department, or a substantial supplement to one, and we write a body of work with you over years. Everything else on this site—the chapters, the artifacts, the thesis, the Scale layer—describes a partnership. None of it applies to a project.

## Projects

A project is scoped, priced, and finished. It has a start, a defined deliverable, and an end you can see from the beginning.

You get the work, the source files, and a shared folder they live in. You get an address to reach us at while the project runs. What you don’t get is the apparatus built for the long term: no thesis, no monthly review, no measurement layer, no standing weekly call. Those exist to make years of work legible, and a six-week website build doesn’t need them.

That isn’t a lesser service. It’s a different one, and pretending otherwise would mean charging you for machinery you have no use for.

Choose a project when you know what you need and you need it done well. A site that’s five years old. A brand that no longer matches the company. A message you can’t get onto paper. These are real problems with real ends, and a project solves them.

## Several projects in a row

There’s a middle path worth knowing about, and it’s often the best value we offer.

Projects strung together—a message, then a visual identity, then a website—amount to a short partnership. Run that way, the work is usually faster and costs less than the same three projects commissioned separately, because nothing has to be re-briefed and no one has to rebuild context between engagements. It also lets us pivot. Three months in, if the message turns out to be different from what everyone assumed, we change course rather than delivering a website built on an answer we no longer believe.

The practical difference is how it’s structured. A sequence of separate projects locks scope at each invoice, so anything new becomes a change order. Run as a short partnership, each month simply buys the next thirty days of work.

There’s no contract either way. You’re never signing up for a term, and if you stop, the payments stop. We would rather earn the next month than hold you to a year.

## Partnerships

A partnership is a different relationship, and the difference is not volume.

We hold a standing weekly call with most partners, and in practice we’re in contact daily. We sit inside your business rather than beside it—we know what’s launching, who just joined, which deal went cold and why. That’s what lets the work be specific rather than generic, and it’s the reason a partnership produces something a project can’t.

It starts with a [thesis](/method/thesis), an argument for why the work should exist that can be proven wrong. It moves through three chapters, each closing with an [artifact](/method/vocabulary). It carries a [measurement layer](/scale/overview) from the first day. And it’s measured monthly against the claim we opened with.

The honest framing: a partnership is the right answer when marketing is an ongoing function of your business rather than a task on a list. If you need a thing, buy the thing. If you need the function, that’s this.

## Most of our work is partnerships

Worth saying plainly, because it shapes how we behave.

We build the practice around long relationships rather than a pipeline of projects, and we’d rather write a manuscript with a small number of companies over years than ship a large number of one-off deliverables. Compounding is the entire thesis of the method, and compounding requires time.

That doesn’t make us reluctant about projects. We take them, we do them properly, and plenty of partnerships started as one. It does mean that if a project keeps generating the next question—what do we do with this site now, who’s writing the content, how do we know it’s working—we’ll tell you that the question is really about a function, not another project.

## Moving from one to the other

Project clients ask what’s next more often than not, and there’s no ceremony to it. A partnership starts the way every partnership starts: a discovery conversation and a thesis. Work already done doesn’t get redone, and it usually shortens Chapter 1 considerably, since the message or the identity is already settled.
