A partnership runs on a small number of platforms, and we would rather use a few of them rigorously than add a new one every time a need appears. The list below is the whole client-facing stack. Everything else we run—project tracking, our CRM, the automation plumbing—sits behind the curtain and never asks anything of you.

The subscriptions are ours. While you are a partner, we pay for these tools and you use them at no additional cost. That is deliberate, and the section at the bottom explains what happens to them if the partnership ends.

Most partners only ever log into three or four of these. The rest run quietly underneath.

## Across the whole partnership

### Google Drive

Every file we produce for you lives in a shared folder, organised the same way for every partner: Brand Assets, Content, and Projects. Finished documents, source files, photography, and exports all land there.

You get the link at kickoff and access is tied to whichever email address you give us. If you cannot find the folder, search your inbox for the invite or ask us and we will resend it.

This is the one place worth bookmarking. When you want to know whether something exists yet, it is faster to look than to ask.

### Support

Not a platform, but the most useful address on this page. Anything that is a request for work, a question about a deliverable, or a change of scope goes to [support@snapmarket.co](mailto:support@snapmarket.co).

It puts the request in front of the whole team and gives it a place in the queue rather than living in one person's inbox, where it depends on that person being at their desk. Urgent things still go to your account lead directly.

## Chapter 1 — Story

### Figma

Where design happens. Your visual identity, brand guidelines, social templates, one-pagers, and podcast artwork are all built and stored here.

We send you a view link when there is something to look at. You do not need an account to open it, and you can leave comments directly on the design rather than describing the problem in an email—click the spot, type the note. That is by far the most useful thing you can do with it, because "the logo feels small" attached to the actual logo is a different instruction than the same words in a message.

For most partners this is review-only. If someone on your team needs to produce graphics from the templates themselves, tell us and we will set that up properly.

## Chapter 2 — System

### Framer or Webflow

Your website. We build in Framer by default because sites are fast to load and, more importantly, editable by your team without a developer. Where an existing site or an in-house preference makes Webflow the better call, we use that instead.

You are added as an editor at launch, and we run a short handover session with whoever on your side will make small changes. Most partners never open it and simply send changes to support. Some prefer to fix a typo themselves at nine at night, which is exactly why we set it up that way.

Your domain is separate and is registered to you, not to us.

### Google Analytics, Search Console, and YouTube

These are yours, not ours, and they are worth naming separately for that reason.

All three are registered under your company's Google account. We are added as administrators so we can instrument, measure, and publish. If we ever stop working together, you remove our access and nothing else changes—no migration, no export, no gap in your history.

You can log into any of them any time. Most partners find the [AgencyAnalytics dashboard](/method/tools#agencyanalytics) easier to read, because it puts all of it in one place.

## Chapter 3 — Send

### Descript and Descript Rooms

Two things under one roof.

Descript Rooms is where episodes get recorded. It is a browser link—no software to install—and it records each participant's audio and video locally at full quality, so a wobbly connection during the call does not become a wobbly recording. You will get a link before each session. Open it in Chrome or Safari, allow the microphone and camera, and that is the whole setup.

Descript itself is where the recording gets edited. You almost certainly will not open this one. It is worth knowing the name because it will come up when we talk about turnaround.

One practical note that matters more than any setting: use wired headphones. It eliminates echo, and it is the single biggest improvement available to the audio quality of your show for the price of a cable you already own.

### Transistor

Podcast hosting and distribution. Your episodes are stored here and pushed out to Apple Podcasts, Spotify, and everywhere else a listener might find them. It is also where listener numbers and retention come from.

We handle publishing entirely. You would only log in to look at download figures directly, and those already appear in your monthly reporting.

### Ordinal

Social media planning, scheduling, and—the part that concerns you—approval.

This is the tool you will use most. Every social post we write for you appears here for review before it goes anywhere. You approve, edit, or reject. Nothing publishes without a person on your side saying yes, and that is a hard rule rather than a default setting.

You get an invite by email at kickoff. It takes about five minutes to learn. If a batch is sitting unapproved, the cadence stops, so it is worth putting ten minutes in the calendar for it rather than leaving it to whenever you next remember.

### Loops or Mailchimp

Email. Newsletters and campaign sends are written, built, and sent from here, along with any automated sequences.

Which one you are on depends on where your list already lives. We do not migrate a working list without a reason—the risk to deliverability is real and rarely worth it.

We send on your behalf, so you would typically only log in to look at open and click rates, which also appear in your reporting. If you want to review a send before it goes out, say so and we will route drafts to you.

## Scale

### AgencyAnalytics

Your live dashboard. Website traffic, search performance, social reach, email engagement, and podcast numbers, gathered from every platform above into one screen.

You get a login at kickoff and you can look whenever you want. Nothing on it is hidden from you, including the parts that are not going well.

Worth setting an expectation, though. The dashboard is a live instrument, and live instruments are noisy—a bad Tuesday looks alarming and means nothing. The [monthly audit](/scale/audit) is where the numbers get read properly, in context, with a view attached. If something on the dashboard worries you, ask rather than concluding.

And for the first sixty to ninety days it will be sparse on purpose. We do not make claims about improvement until there is a clean [baseline](/scale/baseline) to measure against.

## Who pays for all this

We do, for as long as you are a partner. The subscriptions above sit on Snapmarket's accounts and are covered by your fee rather than billed on top of it.

That is worth a sentence of explanation, because it is not how every agency does it. Running these tools ourselves means we can keep the stack consistent across partners, upgrade or replace something without asking you to approve a purchase order, and start work on day one rather than after a procurement conversation. It also means the monthly cost of the stack is our problem to manage, not a line item you have to defend internally.

The exceptions are the things that should never be ours. Your domain, your Google properties, your YouTube channel, and your social accounts are registered to you throughout, with us added as users. Those are your identity in the world and they do not belong on our invoice.

## If the partnership ends

Our subscriptions end with the partnership, so continuing on any of these platforms means setting up your own accounts. We tell you which ones you actually need, with links and current pricing, well before your last month rather than after it.

Once your accounts exist, we port everything across—content, settings, configurations, as completely as each platform allows. Some transfers are clean and some are partial, and we will tell you honestly which is which rather than discovering it together afterward.

For the properties that were always yours, there is nothing to move. You remove our access from Google Analytics, Search Console, YouTube, and anywhere else we hold administrative rights, and you are done.

We would rather set this out plainly here than have it arrive as a surprise in a final invoice. A partnership that ends well is worth more to us than one that ends with an argument about logins.
