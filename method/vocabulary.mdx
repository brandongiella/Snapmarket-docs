We do not run accounts. We write manuscripts. That’s because a manuscript has an arc. At any point in a book you know roughly where you are—early, middle, or near the end—and what kind of thing should be happening. Marketing work usually has no such property. It arrives as a stream of tasks with no understood shape, which is why so many companies cannot say whether their marketing is going well, only whether it is going. The chapters give the work a shape in which you can locate yourself: E.g., “We are in Chapter 2. This is what comes next.”

A manuscript also has a notion of being finished. Each chapter closes with a single **artifact**, and until that artifact exists the chapter is open. Most agencies have no equivalent, which is how engagements drift for years without anyone able to name what has been completed. We would rather be held to a small number of unambiguous finishes than a long list of ambiguous activities.

The word also sets the relationship. A manuscript has an author and an editor, and in this arrangement you are the author. The conviction is yours, the business is yours, and the story is yours to stand behind for years after we are gone. Our role is editorial—to surface what is already true, shape it into language a market can carry, build the machinery that carries it, and tell you honestly when a chapter is not working. That is a different posture than a vendor executing a brief, and the difference shows up in how much we push back.

## The words we use

We keep the vocabulary small and hold it steady, because language matters.

| Word | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Thesis | The one-page argument for why the partnership should exist, written as a claim that can be wrong |
| Project | One bounded piece of work with a defined end—a website, a visual identity, a messaging workshop. Not part of a manuscript. |
| Partner | A client we write a manuscript with over years, with a standing weekly rhythm and daily contact. |
| Manuscript | The partnership itself—the whole body of work, built over years |
| Chapter | One of the three phases: Story, System, Send |
| Section | An area of work inside a chapter, numbered 1.1, 1.2, and so on |
| Piece | A single unit of delivered work |
| Artifact | The deliverable that signals a chapter is complete |
| Scale | The measurement layer running beneath all three chapters |
| Audit | The monthly thesis review, written as an editor’s letter |
| Prologue and Epilogue | The bookends: starting well, and ending well |

You will see this vocabulary on every surface—in our internal project management system, on your deliverables, in your monthly audit. Nobody is going to quiz you on it. It simply means that when someone says “the Storybook is close” or “we are still in 1.1,” everyone in the room is talking about the same thing.

## A note on slowness

The method is patient on purpose. Content published in month one does not produce pipeline in month two, and we would rather say that out loud at the start than manage around it later. What we can promise in the early months is that the work is compounding and that you can see it compounding, which is what the audit is for.
